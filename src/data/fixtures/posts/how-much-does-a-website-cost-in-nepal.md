The honest answer is that a website in Nepal costs anything from a few thousand rupees to several lakh. The useful answer is that the price follows a small number of decisions, and once you know them you can predict a quote before you ask for one.

The prices below are our own published prices at Joonix Studio. Freelancers can be cheaper and larger agencies more expensive. What matters is what each quote includes.

## What does a website cost in Nepal in 2026?

| Type of site                                   | Setup cost                       | Typical build time |
| ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------ |
| Business website, 5 to 15 pages                | Rs. 15,000 to 35,000             | 2 to 3 weeks       |
| Online shop with online payments               | Rs. 60,000 to 1,50,000           | 4 to 6 weeks       |
| Custom platform with accounts and integrations | Rs. 1,50,000 to 3,00,000 or more | 8 weeks or more    |
| Web or mobile app                              | from Rs. 1,00,000                | 6 to 12 weeks      |

A business website covers who you are, what you offer, your prices and a way to reach you, usually with WhatsApp and an enquiry form. An online shop adds a product catalogue, a cart, checkout with eSewa, Khalti and cards, and order emails. A custom platform adds things like customer accounts, coupons, and connections to your accounting or delivery partners.

## What makes one website cost more than another?

Five things move the price more than anything else:

1. **Page types, not page count.** A product page that is designed once and used for 300 products is one page type. A site with ten very different layouts takes longer than a shop with one product template.
2. **Online payments.** Each payment method needs a merchant account, a tested integration, order emails and a way to handle refunds. Read our guide on [which payments to add first](/blog/esewa-khalti-or-cards).
3. **Custom design.** Adapting a well-made template is quicker than designing every page around your brand. Both can look good. Custom design pays off when your brand is part of what you sell.
4. **Content.** Somebody has to write the text and photograph the products. If you have it ready, the project moves faster. If we write or enter it, the quote includes that time.
5. **Integrations.** Connecting the site to accounting software, a delivery partner or an SMS service adds work for each connection.

## What will I pay every month after launch?

The build is a one-time cost. Running the site is not. Expect these running costs:

- **Domain.** A `.com.np` domain is free for eligible Nepali citizens and businesses. A `.com` renews every year at your registrar's price.
- **Hosting.** A small business site needs very little. A busy shop needs more, and the price grows with traffic.
- **Updates, security and backups.** Software needs updating, and somebody has to notice when something breaks. Our care plans cost Rs. 2,000 per month for Launch sites and Rs. 4,000 to 7,000 for shops on the Scale plan.
- **Payment fees.** Payment providers charge a percentage of each transaction. Ask each provider for its current merchant rate.

A site with no care plan still costs money. It is paid later, usually when a plugin stops working or the site is hacked.

## How do I compare two website quotes?

Put the quotes side by side and check each of these:

- Does the quote list the pages, features and payment methods, or only a total?
- Is the price fixed, or billed by the hour?
- Who owns the domain, the code and the content after launch?
- What does the monthly fee include, and what costs extra?
- How will you see progress, and how often?
- What happens if something breaks in the first month?

If a quote cannot answer these in writing, the low price is not the real price.

## When is a cheaper website the right choice?

If you need one page with your services, your location and a phone number, do not spend Rs. 60,000. A simple site, or even a good Google Business Profile, will do the job until your business needs more.

Spend more when the site has to earn money: taking orders, booking appointments or bringing in enquiries from people who have never heard of you. That is when speed, search visibility and a checkout that works on every phone pay for themselves.
