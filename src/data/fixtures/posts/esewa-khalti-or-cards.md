Adding a payment method to an online shop is a business decision before it is a technical one. Each method has paperwork, fees and support work behind it. Adding all of them on day one makes launch slower and rarely sells more.

Here is how we help clients choose, and the order we usually recommend.

## Which payment methods do Nepali buyers use online?

Four methods cover almost every order we see:

- **Cash on delivery.** The buyer pays the rider. It builds trust with first-time customers, but it costs you when orders are refused at the door.
- **Digital wallets.** eSewa and Khalti are the two names most buyers know. The buyer is sent to the wallet, approves the payment and comes back to your shop.
- **Bank QR and mobile banking.** Many buyers pay by scanning a QR code with their bank's app. On a website this usually means showing a QR code at checkout and confirming the payment afterwards.
- **Cards.** Debit and credit cards through a bank payment gateway. Essential for customers abroad, less common for everyday local orders.

## Should I keep cash on delivery?

Yes, at launch. Many buyers will not pay in advance to a shop they have not used before, and removing cash on delivery can cost you those first orders.

You can reduce the risk of refused orders without removing it:

1. Confirm large orders by phone before dispatch.
2. Ask for a small advance on high-value or custom items.
3. Offer a small discount for paying online, so buyers have a reason to switch.

## eSewa or Khalti: which should come first?

Ask your customers. Check which wallet appears most in your shop's existing payments, or ask in your Instagram stories. If there is no clear winner, start with the one whose merchant onboarding you can finish first.

The technical work for both is similar. Once the checkout is built for one wallet, adding the second is a much smaller job, so this decision is about timing, not cost.

## When do card payments make sense?

Add cards when one of these is true:

- You sell to customers outside Nepal, including Nepalis abroad sending gifts home.
- Your average order is large enough that buyers prefer a card to a wallet.
- Your customers are businesses that pay with company cards.

Card payments need a merchant agreement with a bank or a payment gateway, and every payment should pass 3-D Secure verification to reduce fraud. Ask about fees, the settlement currency and how long payouts take before you sign.

## What does it take to add a payment method?

Every method follows the same steps:

1. **Merchant account.** Business registration, a PAN, a bank account in the business name, and the provider's forms.
2. **Test credentials.** The provider gives you a test environment to build against.
3. **Integration.** The checkout sends the buyer to the provider and handles the response. The important part is on the server: the shop must confirm each payment with the provider directly, never trust the browser's word for it.
4. **Order emails and statuses.** Paid, failed and refunded orders each need a clear message to the buyer and to your team.
5. **Refunds.** Decide who issues refunds and how, before the first one is needed.

## What order do we recommend?

For most new shops in Nepal:

1. **At launch:** cash on delivery plus one wallet.
2. **In the first few months:** the second wallet, once customers ask for it.
3. **When the numbers support it:** bank QR and cards, especially if you sell abroad.
